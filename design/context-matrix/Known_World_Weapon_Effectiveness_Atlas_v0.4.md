# Known World — Historical Weapon Effectiveness Atlas

**Working draft v0.4 · 9 October 2026**  
**Purpose:** Historical and old-school-referee analysis plus expanded twelve-weapon die calibration and optional Contextual Weapon Damage rule proposal. **Not an implemented ruleset; no game code modified.**

## Design premise

A published BECMI weapon damage die is the weapon's *ordinary melee effectiveness*, not an invariant measurement of kinetic energy or wound severity. The weapon's effectiveness changes according to range, attack path, target properties, physical surroundings, fighting control, and formation. The referee—not a comprehensive deterministic matrix—judges the circumstances. The eventual optional dice chart would turn that judgment into bounded damage dice without stacking many independent bonuses.

**Source discipline:** Baseline damage/weapon labels below are transcribed from the *Rules Cyclopedia*, Chapter 4, Weapons Table. Historical tendencies are interpretive design hypotheses informed by museum materials cited at the end. The tables are **not** claims of historical performance measurements or printed BECMI combat rules. Explicit BECMI rules and combat maneuvers take precedence, including set spear vs. charge, weapon mastery when enabled, missile cover/range, and published polearm handling.

## A. Seven physical dimensions

1. **Reach and fighting-measure envelope:** effective at *Extended* (held outside typical sword distance), *Normal* (ordinary armed exchange), *Close* (crowded weapon fighting), or *Grapple* (body-to-body control). These are analytical labels, not new engine distances. The engine may retain Close/Normal/Far as its three existing melee groups; a grapple is an established condition inside the Close measure.
2. **Clearance requirements:** swinging arc (lateral and overhead); straight-line thrusting lane; shaft maneuvering/turning space. A long spear can be effective in a narrow straight passage but unusable in an obstructed chamber.
3. **Damage-delivery mechanisms:** cut/chop; thrust/puncture; concentrated blunt impact; hooking/displacement; leverage with haft/hilt. A weapon can have multiple mechanisms.
4. **Target interaction:** vulnerable flesh; textile/padding; mail; rigid plate; thick natural armor; wood and brittle structures. Armor affects BECMI AC already; no automatic double count for the same defensive property.
5. **Handling and recovery:** redirection between lines, changing grip, defensively recovering after an attack. Head-heavy does not automatically mean historically clumsy; geometry, grip, and training all matter.
6. **Control and defense:** warding and threat; parry, bind, hook; displace equipment; mechanically establish control. Do not award automatic disarms or knockdowns just because a weapon has a hook.
7. **Deployment and coordination:** hand requirements, shield compatibility, supporting allies, formation, mounted use, carry and readiness. Two hands alone do not imply access to the large-weapon damage ceiling (e.g., the staff remains BECMI 1d6).

## B. Circumstances assessed separately

- **Distance:** Extended / Normal / Close / Grapple, with actual context controlling the meaning.
- **Space:** open; swing-obstructed; straight thrust lane; low ceiling; shaft confined; cluttered.
- **Target:** size, anatomy, exposed/covered body regions, soft/flexible armor, rigid armor, wooden structure, or shield.
- **Opponent:** moving/charging, defending normally, shield deployed, weapons engaged/bound, off-balance, controlled/restrained, helpless.
- **Formation:** individual, line, second rank, flank exposed, supported by allies, mounted.

The governing judgment is **overall weapon suitability in the established scene**, not an arithmetic sum of five bonuses.

The chart does not treat all 'hard' targets alike. A rigid steel breastplate, a chain-mail hauberk, a wooden door, a stone statue, and an enormous carapace require different reasoning.

**Armor caution:** BECMI AC already captures the difficulty of landing a damaging hit on a protected opponent. Do not apply an additional generic plate-mail damage reduction automatically. An exceptional weapon-specific outcome must have evidence beyond the fact that the target wears armor. A rigid metal plate is not equivalent to a wooden door.

## F. Crossing tests — the atlas must make these comparisons possible

| Encounter is actually established | Expected suitability winner | Key reason | Non-damage consequence to consider |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary armed duel at typical distance | Sword relative to dagger/spear/short sword | broad ordinary attack and defensive repertoire | normally no situational modifier at all |
| Open thrusting lane, spear point holds an approach | Spear relative to sword/dagger | longer effective threat and point delivery | engagement/movement control |
| Narrow *straight* corridor, spear holds frontal line | Spear relative to wide-swinging axe | thrust unobstructed while lateral swing hindered | corridor may negate flanking |
| Narrow winding chamber, shafts cannot be turned | Sword/short sword relative to polearm | geometry and handling | some moves simply impossible |

| Close fighting with NO useful swing arc | Thrust-capable sword/dagger relative to hand axe | weapon can work on a straighter path | handle may remain useful but not full cut |
| Dominant body control with accessible target | Dagger relative to sword/polearm | excellent short-line thrust | control must have been gained, not assumed |
| Armored close duel with suitable poleaxe | Poleaxe relative to ordinary edge-cutting sword | hammer/pick/point design, leverage | armor gap targeting and published mechanics govern |

| Large exposed opponent in clear open ground | Appropriate polearm / two-handed sword | reach and committed blows | large anatomy and target access matter |
| Chopping timber or a wooden door | Axe relative to sword | edge shape and chopping geometry | object damage/time more relevant than combat hit points |

**Threshold:** A niche counts as real only if its relevant circumstances can reasonably be achieved in adventure play and it gives meaningful tactical advantage, not just flavorful narration.

## H. Referee procedure (proposed, minimal)

1. Establish the facts: weapon in hand; target; distance; room for a specific technique; target defenses; who has control; relevant formation. Unknown environmental facts stay unknown—do not invent a low ceiling or armored weakness to force a modifier.
2. Identify the decisive weapon affordance or restriction. Multiple relevant circumstances lead to **one overall suitability judgment**, not stacked bonuses.
3. Apply a governing published BECMI mechanic first (attack, AC, charge, weapon mastery, cover, missile ranges, special immunities, etc.).
4. If a meaningful weapon-specific advantage remains, choose **one** suitable consequence: altered damage die *or* hit modifier *or* positional/handling consequence. Prefer ordinary damage when in doubt. No double counting.
5. Record notable unusual ruling with its triggering physical conditions; do not turn it into a universal weapon bonus. JavaScript rolls and persists effects; AI proposes interpretation only.

## I. Pass 3: five-weapon contextual dice calibration (NEW v0.2)

### I.1 Non-negotiable anchors and measurement

Published *Rules Cyclopedia* weapon dice are retained as the normal *damage on a successful hit*: dagger **1d4**, ordinary sword **1d8**, hand axe **1d6**, spear **1d6**, poleaxe **1d10**. They are *not* experimentally measured wound potentials. The proposed contextual ceilings are game-design hypotheses and deliberately conditional:

| Weapon | Worst **usable** blow | Published normal | Highest proposed situational die | Peak requires |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dagger | 1d2 | 1d4 | **1d10** | established dominant clinch control **and** a vulnerable target accessible to the point |
| Sword (ordinary arming sword) | 1d4 | 1d8 | **1d8** | ordinary open or moderately constrained armed melee already suits it; no gratuitous positive step |
| Hand axe | 1d2 | 1d6 | **1d10** | useful chopping arc against exposed target after achieving a significant positional/shield opening |
| Spear | 1d2 | 1d6 | **1d10** | retains active effective point distance, clear thrust lane and positional control |
| Poleaxe | 1d2† | 1d10 | **2d6** | head/point, leverage and adequate space fully usable against a suitably accessible target |

† A poleaxe has several effective **short-grip** and haft techniques; 1d2 is **not** a penalty for every close fight. It is the fallback only if an actual, highly constrained damaging contact is still possible. When both hands, weapon, and opponent are pinned and no viable attack exists, the weapon cannot attack as declared. A close-trained poleaxe wielder can still be dangerous if the weapon remains free.

### I.2 Single progression; two *operating* power bands

The two conceptual power bands are **not** the same as two automatic modifiers or a universal one-hand/two-hand classification:

- **Compact/general arms:** `1d2 → 1d4 → 1d6 → 1d8 → 1d10` (dagger, sword, hand axe, spear). Each has a different ceiling and a different baseline location; an arming sword stops at 1d8.
- **Heavy force-delivery arms:** `1d4 → 1d6 → 1d8 → 1d10 → 1d12 → 2d6` (poleaxe in this pass). A weapon can temporarily fall below its usual band's bottom when a restricted improvised contact is all that remains.

| Die | Mean on successful hit |
|---|---:|
| 1d2 | 1.5 |
| 1d4 | 2.5 |
| 1d6 | 3.5 |
| 1d8 | 4.5 |
| 1d10 | 5.5 |
| 1d12 | 6.5 |
| 2d6 | 7.0 |

Important: `2d6` is **not** the same distribution as `1d12`; its average is higher by 0.5 and its minimum is 2. We use `2d6` deliberately as a high but modestly increased peak, not as a claim that it simulates an exact impact. Do not extend this ladder automatically to RC multi-die attacks (e.g., heavy crossbow 2d4), plus-value dice (1d8+1), monster damage, or magical/critical multipliers.

### I.3 Six controlled encounter comparisons

**Reading convention:** Each row describes a distinct *established* scene; the columns compare resulting dice **only if the weapon's attempted attack is legal and a meaningful hit is physically possible**. These are encounter-level judgments, **not automatic by range or armor tag**. The winner is the highest per-hit average; ties are shown if they occur. Combatants' hit chances, shields/AC, initiative, mastery, morale, action costs, and other factors also affect which equipment is advantageous overall.

| Established situation | Dagger | Sword | Hand axe | Spear | Poleaxe | Highest average |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **S1. Ordinary melee:** all can freely use an ordinary technique at their accepted engagement | 1d4 | 1d8 | 1d6 | 1d6 | **1d10** | Poleaxe |
| **S2. Crowded shield contact:** hand axeman has *already created an opening* to deliver a committed chop; a long poleaxe's shaft is physically obstructed | 1d4 | 1d8 | **1d10** | 1d4 | 1d6 | Hand axe |
| **S3. Straight constricted approach:** spear wielder has *established* maximum useful point threat; restricted lateral head movement reduces other options, but all depicted attacks remain possible | 1d2 | 1d6 | 1d4 | **1d10** | 1d8 | Spear |
| **S4. Dominant clinch:** dagger wielder has effective control and point access; other weapons have only constrained **viable** contacts | **1d10** | 1d4 | 1d2 | 1d2 | 1d2 | Dagger |
| **S5. Cluttered low-ceiling chamber:** a straight sword thrust is viable, while axe chopping and long-shaft deployment are restricted; not a grapple | 1d6 | **1d8** | 1d4 | 1d4 | 1d4 | Sword |
| **S6. Open reach against large exposed creature:** all are legally engaged, the poleaxe has clear leverage and suitable target access | 1d4 | 1d8 | 1d8 | 1d10 | **2d6** | Poleaxe |

**S3 is intentionally a demanding test.** A historical poleaxe can thrust with its top spike in a straight passage. It should *not* suffer `1d8` simply because a rule calls it a poleaxe. This example assumes an actual difference in the encounter: e.g., the longer spear has preserved a point-only line at the weapon's optimal measure while the shorter poleaxe must make a less powerful constrained action. If that difference is **not present**, use the poleaxe's usual `1d10` (or disallow an out-of-reach attack), even if this means the spear does not win per-hit damage. Reach control, shield compatibility and formation value can distinguish the spear without arbitrary damage equalization.

**S2 is likewise conditional.** Axes do not gain `1d10` merely because an opponent carries a shield. The opening must have been established, and a poleaxe in an unconstrained position may be better than the hand axe; its head can also hook and chop. The scene purposefully distinguishes one-handed access in crowded shield combat, rather than inventing an intrinsic anti-shield damage property.

**S4 is not automatic at Close range.** Existing melee measure *Close* does **not** prove grappling; grappling does **not** prove control; control does **not** prove target access. If the control state is absent, the dagger does not receive `1d10`. Use the governing combat/wrestling procedure where implemented; otherwise a bounded referee ruling with explicitly recorded effects must establish it.

**S5 is not automatic against every two-handed sword or poleaxe.** An arming sword's thrust is assumed available here, while the particular longer shafts are geometrically prevented from their normal strokes. Change the room description and the ruling changes.

### I.4 Crossing criteria — why this is better than a universal 'favorable +1 die'

- **Dagger beats every other reference weapon on actual per-hit damage in S4**, but its requirement (dominant control plus vulnerable access) is significantly harder than simply closing to melee.
- **Sword beats the hand axe, spear and poleaxe in S5** because a straighter, compact attack remains possible. It need not reach `1d10` to have an important niche.
- **Hand axe beats sword and poleaxe in S2** after exploiting a close shield opening and adequate chop clearance. It remains behind the sword in S1.
- **Spear beats sword and poleaxe in S3** *only when* the longer effective point threat and weapon restrictions are actually real. In other circumstances its advantage may be **first engagement/defense**, not damage superiority.
- **Poleaxe beats all four in S6**, but cannot automatically retain `2d6` once its leverage, target access or freedom of attack changes.

This is about **conditional niche supremacy**, not claiming that five weapons have equal lifetime value, equal overall hit frequency, or equal odds of winning a fight. A sword-and-shield fighter may survive more exchanges than a poleaxe wielder even though the latter's successful ordinary hit has a higher damage die.

### I.5 The armor/target test is a *separate hypothesis*, not an automatic matrix column

Target suitability deserves explicit referee attention, but **BECMI Armor Class already represents difficulty landing an effective damaging hit**. Do **not** deduct sword damage merely because a foe wears mail or plate, then also use the foe's improved AC, without a separate substantive reason. Similarly, do not automatically give the poleaxe extra damage merely because it faces plate.

A cautious trial question is: *When a legally successful hit is already established, is there a specific weapon–target interaction, not already represented by AC, that makes the severity of that hit different?* Consider a poleaxe hammer face, pointed pick, a wood-chopping axe head, vulnerable creature anatomy, or a target described as brittle or structurally unusual. If no distinct interaction can be described, **keep the published damage die**.

Historical caveat: real armored combat could involve wrestling and striking armor gaps, and historical poleaxes were multi-purpose with hammer, blade, spike and haft options. A sword could be regripped, so 'sword vs. plate = ineffective' is not a defensible universal rule. Distinguish a Viking single-handed axe from a Dane axe and a hammer-headed poleaxe. These are weapon-form distinctions, not numeric evidence for exact game dice.

### I.6 BECMI procedure and Known World integration safeguards

1. **Authoritative BECMI rule first:** if a published rule already covers the event (set spear vs. charge, weapon mastery, lance charge, shield AC, target-specific immunity or special attack), **apply it instead of duplicating its benefit** with an extra die step. If a published technique has a specified damage rule, use that rule.
2. **Check the actual scene:** equipped exact weapon form, target, effective point/strike line, clearance, opponent defenses, engagement and relative control. The AI **must not invent** ceiling height, bound weapons, shield displacements, restraints, or special vulnerabilities.
3. **Make a single adjudication:** ordinary published die by default; if one decisive unrepresented circumstance clearly matters, select the contextually justified alternative. Avoid adding a new hit penalty *and* reduced damage for exactly the same difficulty unless independently justified.
4. **Respect attack legality:** when the weapon cannot reach or cannot make any viable damaging contact, do **not** pretend that `1d2` permits an attack. Deny the requested technique, or allow a different physically possible one with its proper consequences.
5. **Keep the digital referee bounded:** AI may propose `{weapon,ordinaryDie,contextDie,decisiveFact,alreadyCoveredByRule}`; JavaScript verifies established facts and actual action legality, rolls damage and persists the decision. The current baseline engine is not assumed to have complete grip/control/clearance fields. If it cannot verify a critical fact, do **not** silently grant an exceptional die.
6. **Consistency audit:** document brief precedents for materially similar adjudications; do not generate permanent universal bonuses from a single precedent. Expose short reasons to the player where the character would understand them.

## K. Pass 4 — expanded weapon calibration (v0.3, 9 October 2026)

**Scope:** Retain Pass 3 and its six five-weapon situations unchanged; add seven newly calibrated weapons (**short sword, mace, war hammer, two-handed battle axe, two-handed sword, halberd, pike**) to the original five anchors. The twelve-weapon table below is the **current** provisional envelope. Sections I and K are design alternatives, not mutually stacked modifications. No game code has been changed.

### K.1 Source-led distinctions that constrain the design

- **Published BECMI baselines are not estimates of real-world kinetic energy.** RC Chapter 4 confirms short sword `1d6`, mace `1d6`, war hammer `1d6`, two-handed battle axe `1d8`, two-handed sword `1d10`, halberd `1d10`, and pike `1d10`, alongside the earlier five anchors. RC's battle axe explicitly takes two hands; its war hammer description is a medium-shaft broad hammer head and does **not** automatically supply a narrow pick or beak. RC's two-handed sword is also subject to a printed individual-initiative restriction; Known World's alternate initiative/handling policy must take precedence only where explicitly enabled.
- **Halberd versus pike is historically consequential.** The Met's [German halberd](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/25898) identifies an axe blade, upper spike and beak; it also contrasts individually usable halberds with long pikes used in massed formation. A pike can dominate access to the front without delivering more damage than a halberd on every successful hit.
- **The two-handed sword is not a cartoonishly slow axe.** The Met's [fifteenth-century example](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/35388) is described as well balanced and relatively light for its size, capable of cutting and thrusting, including armor-gap attacks.
- **Mace, hammer and poleaxe target mechanisms differ.** [Royal Armouries, Knights and Castles](https://royalarmouries.org/schools/learning-resources/knights-and-castles) describes maces delivering blunt injury without necessarily breaking armor, specialized mace heads, and poleaxes carrying multiple damaging implements. A target's armor label alone is insufficient to justify an extra die change.

### K.2 Twelve-weapon contextual die envelopes

The published **Normal** column is authoritative. The lower and upper bounds are **house-rule proposals**, to be selected only when conditions are independently established. “Restricted” means the *worst still-viable damaging technique*, not permission to strike an unreachable foe. A dagger's high ceiling requires **dominant control and point access**; a sword already performs optimally in ordinary armed melee.

**The proposed ceiling is an upper bound, not an extra award.** Nothing here stacks with published double damage, mastery damage, charge effects, or target-specific weapon vulnerability. A published special damage expression remains authoritative.

### K.4 What target-protection categories can and cannot decide

| Known target fact | Supported AI inference | Does it automatically change the die? |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary person, exposed or generally equipped | Published die remains the encounter baseline | **No** |
| Mail and padding, fully functioning | Edge cuts may be redirected and concentrated blows may matter; actual hit remains determined by BECMI AC | **No** — requires a specific declared/established method or vulnerability |
| Rigid plate and articulated joints | Some hammers, spikes and purpose-built swords are suited to different techniques | **No** — plate's AC is already operative |
| Existing revealed gap/broken plate/controlled exposed joint | Attack path may produce a meaningful *extra* distinction | **Maybe**, if not already rewarded by a hit modifier, mastery maneuver or special rule |
| Large thick-hided creature | Reach and access could favor long point or heavy head | **No** — anatomy, attack route and strike clearance matter |
| Hollow ceramic, brittle stone, wooden objects | Cutting vs. blunt or concentrated impact can matter in ways AC does not capture | **Maybe**, but published object/structural procedures supersede conjectural HP damage |
| Ordinary shield | Shield AC is already applied; an axe or hook might allow a distinct declared manipulation | **No** — shield presence alone is not a damage award |

For historically motivated differences to show up *against ordinary armor* every round, the game would need to intentionally choose a **weapon-versus-armor house rule**; that is a different design decision from these rare contextual adjudications. Do not smuggle it into seemingly neutral damage examples.

### K.5 Referee selection: one adjudication, not seven arithmetic inputs

1. **Default to the printed die** and published combat rules. Apply initiative, morale, shield, AC, reach legality, class limits, charge/set, mastery, and target special rules as implemented.
2. Establish a concrete fiction fact: *a thrust-only lane*, *an already displaced shield*, *room for a committed chop*, *an exposed weak fitting*, *a controlled clinch*, *an open formed pike front*, and so on. Never infer a new gap, restraint, brittle carapace, or ceiling height merely to create a weapon bonus.
3. Ask whether that fact is **already mechanically represented**. If so, prefer no additional effect. Otherwise choose **one** bounded result: a context die; an existing attack adjustment; a maneuver/position; an object consequence; or no viable attack. Do not apply all of them for the same causal fact.
4. Choose the **closest justified die within that exact weapon's envelope**. “Ideal” means genuinely unusual or exact-fit conditions, not the highest die merely because the target is soft or the spear is at Far measure.
5. Preserve state discipline: the AI makes a recommendation and explains the operative fact; JavaScript checks authoritative weapon, target, and persistent conditions and performs the roll. No AI-only invention of grappling control or combat positions. Where the current engine does not track the decisive fact, keep normal dice until the action/state has been established.

### K.6 Known World integration: data that must be available before any engine changes

The earlier Known World combat description tracks 3D coordinates, influence spheres, shared `Close / Normal / Far` melee measures, weapons, and target size/protection. **That does not by itself establish** full room clearance, a particular thrust line, shield displacement, restraint quality, weapon grip, or a specific vulnerable joint.

Minimum additional *referee evidence* (not new numeric weapon stats):

- **Equipped exact weapon form** and whether the grip/use is one- or two-handed; avoid inventing a beak on RC's generic war hammer.
- **Proven surroundings:** terrain/room dimensions only where keyed or observed; obstacles and whether the declared attack path is physically clear.
- **Engagement facts:** who contacts whom and the common melee band; **separately**, whether binding, grappling, or established dominant control has actually occurred.
- **Visible target facts:** actual shield, armor, material, exposed region, stated unusual vulnerability, and published monster special handling.
- **Persistent adjudication state:** only the exceptional fact needed to reproduce the ruling (e.g. `shield_displaced=true`, `control=dominant`, `clearance=thrust_only`), and only if validated and maintained as the circumstances change. These are illustrative data labels, **not a demand to add them now**.

**Example bounded proposal, not an implemented API:**

```json
{
  "actor": "Fighter A",
  "weapon": "hand axe",
  "target": "Shielded Orc",
  "ordinary_damage": "1d6",
  "suggested_damage": "1d10",
  "decisive_established_fact": "shield displacement succeeded; clear short chop now possible",
  "already_awarded_by_existing_rule": false,
  "referee_explanation": "The short axe has a clean chopping opening."
}
```

If the engine has not actually recorded the shield displacement, it **must reject** the proposal and retain normal damage; the AI's assertion is not itself evidence. It must also respect which combat phase or attack occurs after the successful displacement.

### K.7 Remaining unresolved questions

- **Persistent control states:** Do not invent grappling/weapon binds or automatically treat the engine's Close measure as equivalent to either.
- **Armor specialization versus AC:** Decide deliberately whether the atlas should support *rare method-specific adjustments* only (current default) or an explicit optional weapon/armor interaction toggle. The latter risks recreating *Arms Across Eras*.
- **Published source precedence:** RC blanket two-handed-weapon initiative notes and Known World's explicit alternate phase/handling rules can differ; document the active toggle and never silently import a second penalty.
- **Exceptional dice ladder:** `1d12` (mean 6.5) to `2d6` (mean 7.0) is only a half-point step and changes the distribution, unlike earlier +1-mean steps. This is a conscious provisional choice, not historically calibrated mathematics.
- **Extending to other weapon types:** Additional work remains for staff, javelin, trident, shield weapons, bastard sword (`+1` dice), missile weapons (`2d4` etc.), and monster natural attacks.
- **No automatic balance symmetry:** Short sword can outshine sword in a genuinely cramped passage; mace and war hammer can tie against a brittle object; halberd and poleaxe can tie against a large exposed creature. Do not force every weapon to have a unique numeric victory where historical function overlaps.

## L. Pass 5 — Contextual Weapon Damage (CWD) v0.4 formal proposal (9 October 2026)

**Status:** Optional house-rule specification, **not** code implemented, not published RAW, and not empirically playtested. Sections I/K remain the historical/technical exploratory foundation. The table below is the new *operational* proposal; earlier scenario tables are calibration tests, not mandatory outcome tables.

### L.1 Goal and rule in one paragraph

The published BECMI weapon die represents normal effectiveness in an ordinary encounter. When **established** weapon, target, positioning and environmental facts create a substantive advantage or disadvantage that the existing rules do **not** already reward, the referee selects one bounded **contextual weapon damage die** from this weapon's row. **Do not adjust the BECMI to-hit roll to simulate this same weapon-context distinction.** The attack uses normal BECMI accuracy/AC; after a hit, roll the selected die plus the actor's otherwise applicable damage bonuses. If no decisive fact exists, the ordinary printed die applies. A weapon's ordinary die is not necessarily the midpoint or its maximum. In particular, the arming sword normally peaks at 1d8, while a dagger can sometimes exceed it after obtaining dominance at grappling distance.

### L.2 Five qualitative outcomes

| Rating | Required finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Severely restricted** | Only an awkward but still materially damaging contact is physically possible | Use the severe column; no hit if the action is actually impossible |
| **Disadvantaged** | A verified obstacle, distance, angle, or binding prevents an ordinary effective technique | Use disadvantaged column |
| **Normal** | Ordinary melee, or insufficient evidence of decisive differences | Use printed BECMI base die |
| **Favored** | A substantial *established* advantage permits a special technique, attack path, reach control or target interaction | Use favored column |
| **Exceptional** | An unusually strong specialized opportunity is *established*, usually needing both weapon suitability and secured positional/target access | Use exceptional column, capped by weapon |

These are **alternative judgments**, not independently stacking distance, armor, terrain, size, and control modifiers. **Do not upgrade by a tier merely because a player uses vivid descriptive language.** Prefer Normal when relevant spatial/physical facts are unknown. The historical profiles explain what each weapon needs, not what bonus it always gets.

### L.3 Provisional operating matrix

| Weapon | Severe | Disadvantaged | **Normal (published)** | Favored | Exceptional | Notes on when high-end may apply |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Dagger | 1d2 | 1d2 | **1d4** | **1d6–1d8** | **1d10** | 1d6 for meaningful close access; 1d8 only when controlling leverage exists; 1d10 needs dominant restraint **and** vulnerable point access |
| Short sword | 1d4 | 1d4 | **1d6** | 1d8 | 1d8 | Confined usable thrust/cut where a full-length sword is materially impeded |
| Arming sword | 1d4 | 1d6 | **1d8** | 1d8 | 1d8 | A wide normal envelope is its benefit; no automatic increase past printed die |
| Hand axe | 1d2 | 1d4 | **1d6** | 1d8 | **1d10** | Successful short chopping opening and sufficient arc; a shield in the scene alone is not enough |
| Mace | 1d4 | 1d4 | **1d6** | 1d8 | **1d10** | Independently established impact-vulnerable target, not armor AC alone |
| War hammer (1H) | 1d4 | 1d4 | **1d6** | 1d8 | **1d10** | Concentrated impact or beak geometry **only if the specific hammer has it** |
| Spear | 1d2 | 1d4 | **1d6** | 1d8 | **1d10** | Secured point control, suitable approach, clear thrust lane; printed set-vs-charge takes priority |
| Battle axe (2H) | 1d4 | 1d6 | **1d8** | 1d10 | **1d12** | Full chopping clearance and unusually favorable target/strike path |
| Two-handed sword | 1d6 | 1d8 | **1d10** | 1d10 | **1d12** | Effective full technique with exceptional leverage/access; close grip adaptations remain possible |
| Poleaxe | 1d2 | 1d6 | **1d10** | 1d12 | **2d6** | Suitable head/point technique at effective reach, ample clearance, accessible target |
| Halberd | 1d2 | 1d6 | **1d10** | 1d12 | **2d6** | Effective extended geometry for head/spike/hook and suitable exposed target |
| Pike | 1d2 | 1d6 | **1d10** | 1d10 | **1d12** | Secured, unobstructed frontal approach in useful supporting formation; usually its chief advantage is positional, not damage |

**Dagger favored subcase:** The one interval (`1d6–1d8`) is *not* arbitrary per-hit random choice: use `1d6` for simple secured short-range access, `1d8` only for verified substantial but not dominant controlling leverage, `1d10` only for dominant control plus access. This is a deliberately limited refinement of one weapon. The referee must not equate the melee band **Close** with a grapple, or a grapple with dominant restraint.

**Dice ladder:** 1d2 (mean 1.5); 1d4 (2.5); 1d6 (3.5); 1d8 (4.5); 1d10 (5.5); 1d12 (6.5); 2d6 (7). `2d6` changes the minimum and distribution as well as the mean; it is a deliberate high-end proposal, not a universal increment after d12. The two-handed *family* has access to higher peaks where historically warranted, not because two-handed use always grants a bonus (staff is counterexample).

### L.4 Decision procedure for a single attack

1. **Authority and mode:** If the attack invokes a specific published damage procedure (set spear vs. charge, lance attack, special monster damage, etc.), apply that rule rather than adding a contextual bump for the same circumstance. CWD and full Arms Across Eras matrix mode are **mutually exclusive** weapon damage modes. Existing BECMI to-hit, AC, strength, magic, and explicit special rules remain authoritative.
2. **Snapshot, before the attack roll:** Resolve the actual equipped weapon, target, declared method, engagement/actual reach, effective Close/Normal/Far melee measure (where used), keyed obstruction/clearance, existing control state, target anatomy/material, and any action/phase restrictions. Do not infer a hidden vulnerable joint, control, displaced shield, or room dimension if not established.
3. **Attack legality:** If no viable damaging technique is possible, deny that technique; never use 1d2 as a trick to permit an impossible attack. Players may propose an alternative action (shorten grip, draw dagger, move, exploit another path) if otherwise legal.
4. **Find a meaningful *unrepresented* difference:** Apply the baseline die unless known circumstances make normal physical effectiveness importantly different and the effect is not already handled by RAW AC, attack rolls, movement, positional resolution, mastery, or a monster's special rule.
5. **One rating:** Select Severe / Disadvantaged / Normal / Favored / Exceptional once from the whole established situation. Read the bounded die from the weapon row. Favor the less extreme ruling when facts are uncertain. Use the dagger subcase only if needed.
6. **Resolve:** Freeze the die **before** rolling to hit. Make the ordinary BECMI attack roll. On a hit, JavaScript rolls the selected die and adds otherwise applicable modifiers in the order prescribed by the active BECMI rule. Do not let the AI roll or alter HP. Do not replace extra effects such as poison, magical properties, damage multipliers or mastery damage with a single contextual die.
7. **Explain and preserve:** Give a brief rationale when a substitution is observable, record original die / context die / established decisive fact / active mode / exception, and end the effect when the circumstance changes. Retain short precedents for consistent future rulings. The AI's suggestion is never sufficient evidence for the condition it asserts.

### L.5 Representative rulings and non-rulings

| Situation established by engine/encounter | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Fighter with sword attacks ordinary armed orc at Normal distance | **1d8** | Sword's ordinary baseline is already its effective peak |
| Dagger fighter enters Close melee but controls nothing | **1d4** (usually) | Close is not equal to grapple/control |
| Dagger fighter has a validated clinch with controlling leverage, but not full dominance | **1d8** | Specific dagger favored subcase |
| Dagger fighter secures dominant restraint **and** accessible vulnerable thrust path | **1d10** | Exceptional dagger niche; setup has already occurred |
| Hand axe hits a defender carrying an intact shield, no special opening | **1d6** | Shield's normal effect already contributes to AC |
| Hand axe has secured shield displacement and unobstructed short chop path | **1d8**, possibly **1d10** if particularly powerful opening established | Its concentrated chopping advantage is meaningful and not mere shield ownership |
| Spearman controls the extended point in a real thrust lane | **1d8** | Favored spear context |
| Spearman is *setting against a charge* under the published maneuver | **Published charge rule**, not contextual 1d8/1d10 on top | Avoid duplicate benefit |
| Sword attacking target merely described as plate armored | **1d8** | AC already affects chance of damaging hit; no extra fact established |
| Poleaxe with free head/point, suitable exposed large target and clear leverage | **2d6** if exceptional physical access truly established | Bounded heavy-weapon peak |
| Pike trapped sideways inside fully obstructed room | **No attack with the proposed point**, or viable low-damage haft move if physically available | Attack legality comes before damage |
| Player says 'I strike expertly at a weak spot,' with no mechanical or described advantage | **Printed die** | Adjectives do not establish circumstances |

**Important target-protection design policy:** Armor Class remains the baseline defense model, so ordinary mail or plate alone never applies an automatic contextual die penalty/bonus. Distinct vulnerability or a *declared and achieved* specialized method might change the damage of a hit. This cautious first implementation preserves BECMI combat math. If the design later intentionally wants weapon-versus-armor modifiers on most attacks, that must be an explicitly different option and audited for the interaction with AC.

### L.6 Scope boundaries

- v0.4 **covers these twelve ordinary single-die mundane melee weapons only**; exact class restrictions, weapon readiness, secondary attacks, missile combat, throwing and cross-scale combat remain as separately implemented.
- A weapon with `+1` *printed* base damage, e.g. a bastard sword's `1d8+1`, or with multiple dice such as a heavy crossbow's `2d4`, needs its own row before contextual substitution is enabled. Do not flatten its printed dice automatically.
- Strength, magic and other ordinary damage additions are applied after base die substitution as usual; the weapon's ceiling caps **the base die only**, not total combat damage.
- Weapon Mastery can already change damage and grant maneuvers. For initial integration, do not stack CWD onto a mastery-specific damage benefit from the same attack feature. Test mastery interactions separately before enabling them.
- Successful weapon-control states (grapple, bind, shield displacement, etc.) must be established through an existing or deliberately implemented procedure. **Do not silently create a new grapple check or bonus roll.**
- Contextual damage is optional. Existing BECMI RAW and Arms Across Eras profiles remain available as independent modes; this proposal does not delete any completed engine work.

Existing scenes that lack measured clearance, binding, or control information default to **Normal** rather than fabricate a favorable condition. This is a proposed interface, not evidence that the current Known World production build already exposes all needed state.

### L.8 Implementation checkpoints and acceptance tests

**Checkpoint A — rules toggle/data only:** add `RAW`, `CONTEXTUAL`, `AAE` modes without modifying any existing mode; store 12 profiles, printed baselines, allowed results and human-readable signature strengths. Test the engine exactly preserves RAW/AAE outcomes and base damage when CONTEXTUAL has no proven difference.

**Checkpoint B — bounded adjudication:** provide the AI with a compact scene/weapon profile and allow a context rating proposal. Validate identifiers and permitted dice, check supplied evidence against real engine facts, snapshot the result before the attack roll, and send only the selected die to the deterministic damage roller. Fail closed to printed damage on missing evidence, network failure, ambiguity or invalid proposal.

**Checkpoint C — minimum usable control state:** only where an existing action has a valid procedure, persist factual controls such as confirmed successful grapple level, displaced shield, and temporary attack-path obstruction. Do **not** treat Close melee as an already-established grapple. Preserve declared weapon/movement/action economy.

**Checkpoint D — focused combat and regression tests:** establish that the ordinary sword is 1d8; a dagger remains 1d4 merely by entering Close; control can produce 1d8 and dominance 1d10; hand axe shield case has no passive bonus; spear cannot receive the same charge advantage twice; enclosed poleaxe attack with no path is denied; ordinary plate armor does not auto-penalize sword damage; context mode respects attack rolls, Strength/magic, save/load, special monster damage, and RAW/AAE mode isolation.

**Checkpoint E — playtest balance:** compare number of attacks/rounds and total delivered damage across ordinary short fights, plus costs of acquiring/maintaining favorable positions. A bounded *single-hit* die does not guarantee bounded **encounter-level** advantage if a favorable situation is granted for free every round.

**Final design principle:** *Specific BECMI rule first. Actual established context second. One bounded die substitution third. Normal damage whenever the referee lacks enough evidence.*
